An Excel-based sales performance dashboard developed as part of my Data Analysis learning journey. The project analyzes revenue, profit, payment behaviour, delivery performance, departmental performance, regional performance, and customer revenue using interactive dashboards and visualizations.
 Sales Performance Analysis Dashboard — Excel
Project Overview

This project is a Sales Performance Analysis Dashboard created as part of my practical Data Analysis training.

The objective was to transform sales data into an interactive dashboard that makes it easier to understand business performance across different areas, including revenue, profit, profit margin, customers, payment methods, departments, regions, and delivery performance.

As a student learning Data Analysis, this project gave me an opportunity to practice not only technical Excel skills but also the process of asking:

"What is the data telling us, and what business decision could this information support?"

This is one of the projects I am using to document my growth as I continue developing my skills and building my Data Analytics portfolio.

 Key Insights
1. Strong overall profitability

The dashboard shows:

Total Revenue: ₦318,291 for Florida (based on the screenshot attached beneath)you can checkout the project file to see for other region.
Total Profit: ₦163,134
Overall Profit Margin: 53%

This indicates that profit represents approximately 53% of the recorded revenue in the dataset for Florida

For a real business analysis, I would investigate the cost components behind this margin to understand what is driving profitability.

2. Transfer was the dominant payment method

The dashboard shows:

Payment Type	Revenue	Profit
Transfer	₦207,590	₦102,443
Credit Card	₦110,701	₦60,691

Transfer generated substantially more revenue than credit-card payments in the displayed data.

This could be useful for understanding customer payment preferences and evaluating whether payment method is associated with differences in transaction value or profitability.

3. Customer revenue was not evenly distributed

Conor Wise generated the highest revenue among the customers displayed, at approximately ₦117,764, followed by David Rasmussen at about ₦67,429.

This suggests that a relatively small number of customers contribute a significant portion of the recorded revenue.

A business could therefore investigate:

Who the highest-value customers are
What products/services they purchase
Their purchasing frequency
How to retain high-value customers
4. Delivery performance varied considerably

The dashboard records delivery-related values ranging from approximately 3 days to 79 days.

That is a substantial difference.

The business should investigate the transactions with longer delivery periods to determine whether the delays are associated with:

Region
Customer
Department
Payment method
Order type
Operational processes

This could potentially reveal an operational area requiring attention.

5. Operations contributed significantly to the displayed performance

The departmental section shows Operations with approximately:

Revenue: ₦318,291
Profit: ₦163,134

This suggests that Operations is a major contributor within the currently displayed dashboard selection.

 Recommendations

Based on the analysis, I would recommend that the business:

1. Investigate delivery delays

Analyze the orders with the longest delivery times and identify the underlying causes.

2. Monitor high-value customers

Customers generating substantial revenue should be analyzed further to understand their purchasing behaviour and retention opportunities.

3. Analyze payment behaviour

Since transfers account for a larger share of the displayed revenue, management could investigate why customers prefer this payment method.

4. Track profitability alongside revenue

High revenue does not automatically mean high profitability. Future analysis should continue comparing revenue, costs and profit at customer, product, department and regional levels.

5. Expand the dataset

A larger dataset covering more customers, regions, departments and longer periods would allow for stronger trends and more reliable conclusions.
